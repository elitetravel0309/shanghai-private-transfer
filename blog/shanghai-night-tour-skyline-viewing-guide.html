Shanghai Night Tour & Skyline Viewing Guide 2026

September 7, 2026 11 min read By Shanghai Private Transfer

Shanghai at night is one of the most spectacular urban light shows on Earth. As the sun sets, the city transforms into a glittering wonderland — the iconic Pudong skyline with its three supertall towers, the historic colonial buildings of The Bund illuminated in golden light, the Huangpu River reflecting a thousand colors, and the neon-lit streets of Nanjing Road stretching as far as the eye can see. A Shanghai night tour is an essential experience for any visitor to the city.

But Shanghai's best night views are spread across the city — from The Bund promenade to the observation decks of Lujiazui, from rooftop bars to river cruise boats, from illuminated gardens to bustling night markets. Navigating between them after dark can be challenging, especially with tired feet after a full day of sightseeing. A private car with driver is the perfect solution for a Shanghai night tour.

In this complete guide, we cover the best night views and evening activities in Shanghai, suggested night tour itineraries, and why a private car is the ideal way to experience Shanghai after dark.

Best Night Views & Viewpoints in Shanghai

1. The Bund (外滩) — The Classic Skyline View

  • Location: Huangpu District, along Zhongshan East 1st Road
  • Best time to visit: 7:00 PM – 10:00 PM (all buildings illuminated)
  • Admission: Free
  • What to see: The most iconic view in Shanghai — the Pudong skyline across the Huangpu River, featuring the Oriental Pearl Tower, Shanghai Tower, Jin Mao Tower, and SWFC. The 52 historic colonial buildings along The Bund are also beautifully illuminated. The Bund light show (every evening, with music and synchronized building lights) is a must-see.
  • Best photo spots: The northern end near Garden Bridge (Waibaidu Bridge) for fewer crowds; the central promenade near the Customs House for the classic postcard view; the southern end near Nanpu Bridge for a wider angle.
  • Tip: Arrive before 7 PM to secure a good spot along the railing. The Bund can be very crowded on weekends and holidays — visit on a weekday for a more relaxed experience. Your driver can drop you off at the best entrance and pick you up at a convenient location.

2. Shanghai Tower Observation Deck (上海中心大厦观光厅)

  • Location: Pudong, Lujiazui, 501 Yincheng Middle Road
  • Best time to visit: 30 minutes before sunset through night (see both day and night views)
  • Admission: ¥180 (adults), ¥90 (children 1.0–1.4m), free (children under 1.0m)
  • What to see: The highest observation deck in the world at 561 meters (118th floor). 360-degree panoramic views of the entire Shanghai metropolitan area, with glass floors in some sections for a thrilling vertical view. On clear nights, you can see the city lights stretching to the horizon. The world's fastest elevator (18 m/s) takes you to the top in under 55 seconds.
  • Tip: Book tickets in advance online to skip the ticket queue. Visit 30 minutes before sunset to see the city transition from day to night — the most magical time. Bring a jacket as it can be cool at that height, even in summer.

3. Jin Mao Tower Skywalk (金茂大厦云中漫步)

  • Location: Pudong, Lujiazui, 88 Century Avenue
  • Best time to visit: Evening for night views
  • Admission: ¥120 (observation deck), ¥388 (Skywalk experience)
  • What to see: The 88th-floor observation deck (340 meters) offers stunning views of the Pudong skyline and The Bund across the river. The unique "Skywalk" experience lets you walk on a glass-bottomed catwalk outside the building (with safety harness) for an adrenaline-pumping view of the city below. The interior atrium (a 28-story spiral) is also architecturally stunning.
  • Tip: The Skywalk experience is weather-dependent and may be closed on windy or rainy days. Book in advance if you want to try it. The regular observation deck is open regardless of weather.

4. Shanghai World Financial Center (SWFC) Observation Deck (环球金融中心观光厅)

  • Location: Pudong, Lujiazui, 100 Century Avenue
  • Best time to visit: Evening for night views
  • Admission: ¥140 (94th + 97th + 100th floors), ¥100 (94th floor only)
  • What to see: The 100th-floor observation deck (474 meters) features a 55-meter-long glass-bottomed skywalk — the highest of its kind in the world. Look straight down through the glass floor to the streets 474 meters below. The 97th floor has an open-air (glass-enclosed) observation area, and the 94th floor has a large exhibition space and gift shop.
  • Tip: The SWFC is less crowded than Shanghai Tower, making it a good choice for a more relaxed observation deck experience. The glass floor on the 100th floor is a unique photo opportunity — not for the faint of heart!

5. Oriental Pearl Tower (东方明珠广播电视塔)

  • Location: Pudong, Lujiazui, 1 Century Avenue
  • Best time to visit: Evening for night views
  • Admission: ¥160 (upper sphere + lower sphere + Shanghai History Museum), ¥220 (all levels including space capsule)
  • What to see: Shanghai's most iconic landmark, with its distinctive sphere-on-sphere design. The upper sphere (263 meters) offers 360-degree views of the city, with a glass-floor section for thrilling vertical views. The lower sphere has a roller coaster simulator and other attractions. The Shanghai History Museum at the base is an excellent cultural exhibit. The tower itself is beautifully illuminated at night and is a key part of the Pudong skyline.
  • Tip: The Oriental Pearl Tower is very popular and can be crowded. Book tickets in advance and visit on a weekday if possible. The glass floor in the upper sphere is a fun photo spot — lie down for a dramatic "floating over the city" shot.

6. Huangpu River Night Cruise (黄浦江夜游)

  • Location: Various piers along The Bund and Pudong
  • Best time to visit: 7:00 PM – 9:00 PM (all buildings illuminated)
  • Admission: ¥120–¥300 (varies by cruise type and duration)
  • What to see: A boat cruise along the Huangpu River offers a unique perspective on Shanghai's night skyline. See both The Bund's historic buildings and Pudong's modern skyscrapers from the water, with the city lights reflected on the river surface. Cruises range from 30-minute standard tours to 2-hour dinner cruises with live entertainment. Some cruises include an open-air top deck for unobstructed views and photos.
  • Best piers: Shiliupu Pier (十六铺码头, near The Bund), Jinling East Road Pier (金陵东路码头), Lujiazui Pier (陆家嘴码头, near Oriental Pearl Tower)
  • Tip: Book cruise tickets in advance, especially on weekends and holidays. Choose a cruise with an open-air top deck for the best views and photos. Bring a jacket as it can be breezy on the water. Your driver can drop you off at the pier and pick you up after the cruise.

7. Rooftop Bars & Restaurants with a View

  • Location: Various locations in The Bund, Lujiazui, and French Concession
  • Best time to visit: Sunset through night (6:00 PM – 11:00 PM)
  • Admission: Drink minimum or cover charge varies (¥100–¥500 per person)
  • Best rooftop bars:
    • Flair (Ritz-Carlton, IFC): The highest rooftop bar in Shanghai (58th floor), with stunning views of the Pudong skyline and The Bund. Outdoor terrace with fire pits and comfortable seating.
    • Vue Bar (Hyatt on the Bund): 32nd-floor rooftop bar with both indoor and outdoor seating, panoramic views of The Bund and Pudong. Famous for its outdoor hot tubs (yes, really!).
    • Char Bar (Indigo Hotel, The Bund): 29th-floor rooftop bar with unobstructed views of the Pudong skyline. Casual atmosphere, great cocktails, and a popular spot for sunset.
    • TOPS (Banyan Tree, The Bund): Elegant rooftop bar on the 28th floor, with views of The Bund and Pudong. Sophisticated atmosphere, perfect for a special evening.
    • Le Bar (Le Royal Meridien, Nanjing West Road): Rooftop bar with views of the city center and People's Square. More affordable than The Bund rooftop bars.
  • Tip: Most rooftop bars have a dress code (smart casual) and may require reservations, especially on weekends. Prices are high (¥100–¥200 per cocktail), but the views are worth it for a special evening. Your driver can recommend bars based on your budget and preferences, and help with reservations in Chinese.

8. Yu Garden & Old City at Night (豫园夜景)

  • Location: Huangpu District, Old City (near Yuyuan Garden MTR station)
  • Best time to visit: 6:30 PM – 9:30 PM
  • Admission: ¥40 (Yu Garden, open until 5 PM), free (Yu Garden Bazaar, open until 10 PM)
  • What to see: The Yu Garden area transforms into a magical lantern-lit wonderland at night. Traditional Chinese architecture, red lanterns, illuminated pagodas, and the golden Huxinting Teahouse reflected in the pond create a stunning atmosphere. The bazaar is filled with shops, restaurants, and street food vendors. During festivals (especially Chinese New Year and Mid-Autumn Festival), the area hosts elaborate lantern displays that are not to be missed.
  • Tip: The Yu Garden Bazaar is very crowded on weekends and holidays — visit on a weekday evening for a more relaxed experience. Try the local street food: nanxiang xiaolongbao (soup dumplings), tanghulu (candied hawthorn), and Shanghai-style snacks. The Huxinting Teahouse is a beautiful spot for a cup of tea with a view.

9. Nanjing Road Night Walk (南京路步行街夜景)

  • Location: Huangpu District, Nanjing East Road (pedestrian street)
  • Best time to visit: 7:00 PM – 10:00 PM
  • Admission: Free
  • What to see: Nanjing Road at night is a dazzling display of neon lights, LED screens, and bustling crowds. The world's longest shopping street comes alive after dark, with illuminated storefronts, giant video screens, street performers, and thousands of shoppers and tourists. The street is pedestrianized, making it perfect for an evening stroll. Start at The Bund end and walk west to People's Square for the full experience.
  • Tip: Nanjing Road is very crowded at night, especially on weekends. Keep an eye on your belongings and stay with your group. The giant LED screen at the intersection of Nanjing Road and Henan Road is a popular photo spot. Stop for a snack at one of the many food stalls or restaurants along the street.

10. Lujiazui Riverside Promenade (陆家嘴滨江大道)

  • Location: Pudong, along the Huangpu River (opposite The Bund)
  • Best time to visit: 7:00 PM – 10:00 PM
  • Admission: Free
  • What to see: The Lujiazui riverside promenade offers the reverse view of The Bund — looking across the river at the illuminated historic buildings of The Bund, with the Pudong skyscrapers towering behind you. Less crowded than The Bund promenade, with beautiful gardens, walking paths, and several restaurants and cafes with river views. The promenade stretches from the Oriental Pearl Tower in the north to the Shanghai Tower area in the south.
  • Tip: The Lujiazui promenade is much quieter than The Bund, making it perfect for a romantic evening stroll or a relaxed family walk. There are several benches and seating areas with river views. Combine a visit here with dinner at one of the riverside restaurants in Lujiazui.

Tips for a Great Shanghai Night Tour

  1. Book an evening private car — Shanghai's best night views are spread across the city. A 4–6 hour evening private car (6:30 PM–10:30 PM or later) lets you visit multiple viewpoints comfortably, with your driver waiting at each stop.
  2. Time your visit for 7:00 PM – 10:00 PM — All major buildings are fully illuminated during these hours. The sky is dark enough for dramatic photos, and the Bund light show runs every evening. After 10 PM, some buildings start to dim their lights.
  3. Visit Shanghai Tower before sunset — If you plan to visit an observation deck, arrive 30 minutes before sunset to see the city transition from day to night — the most magical time. You'll see both the golden hour glow and the full night illumination.
  4. Bring a jacket — It can be cool and breezy at night, especially on observation decks, rooftop bars, and river cruises. Even in summer, bring a light jacket. In winter, dress warmly — it can be very cold on the water or at height.
  5. Wear comfortable shoes — You'll do a fair amount of walking at The Bund, Nanjing Road, and other viewpoints. Wear comfortable shoes and take breaks in the car between stops.
  6. Ask your driver for the best photo spots — Local drivers know the hidden viewpoints, least-crowded spots, and best angles for photos. They can also help with restaurant reservations and attraction tickets in Chinese.
  7. Combine viewpoints for variety — Don't just visit one spot. A great night tour combines a high-altitude view (observation deck), a river-level view (The Bund or cruise), and a street-level view (Nanjing Road or Yu Garden) for a complete experience.
  8. Avoid weekends and holidays if possible — The Bund, Nanjing Road, and observation decks are very crowded on weekends and Chinese holidays. Visit on a weekday for a more relaxed experience. If you must visit on a weekend, arrive early (before 7 PM) to secure good spots.
  9. Plan for dinner — A night tour works up an appetite. Plan to have dinner either before the tour (6:00 PM) or during a break (8:00–9:00 PM). Your driver can recommend family-friendly or romantic restaurants near each viewpoint.
  10. Don't overschedule — It's better to fully enjoy 2–3 viewpoints than rush through 5. Spend at least 45–60 minutes at each major stop, and leave time for the drive between locations. Your driver can help you plan a realistic itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What time do the Shanghai skyline lights turn on and off?

A: The Pudong skyline and The Bund buildings typically turn on their lights at sunset (around 6:00 PM in winter, 7:00 PM in summer) and turn off around 10:00–11:00 PM. The Bund light show (synchronized music and building lights) runs every evening, usually at 7:00 PM, 8:00 PM, and 9:00 PM (each show lasts about 5–10 minutes). During holidays and special events, the lights may stay on later and there may be additional light shows. Check the latest schedule locally or ask your driver for the current evening's light show times.

Q: Which observation deck has the best night view?

A: It depends on what you're looking for: - Shanghai Tower (561m): The highest and most comprehensive 360° view. Best for seeing the entire metropolitan area stretching to the horizon. - SWFC (474m): The unique glass-floor skywalk and less crowded atmosphere. Best for thrill-seekers and those who want a more relaxed experience. - Jin Mao Tower (340m): The classic Art Deco interior atrium and Skywalk experience. Best for architecture lovers. - Oriental Pearl (263m): The most iconic landmark and glass floor. Best for families and first-time visitors who want the classic Shanghai experience. For most visitors, we recommend Shanghai Tower for the best overall night view, combined with The Bund for the classic ground-level skyline perspective.

Q: Is a Huangpu River night cruise worth it?

A: Yes, absolutely! A Huangpu River night cruise offers a unique perspective that you can't get from any observation deck or promenade. You'll see both The Bund's historic buildings and Pudong's modern skyscrapers from the water, with the city lights reflected on the river surface. The gentle boat ride is relaxing and romantic, and many cruises include dinner or drinks. We recommend a 1-hour standard cruise (¥120–¥180) for the best value, or a dinner cruise (¥250–¥400) for a special occasion. Book in advance, especially on weekends.

Q: Are rooftop bars expensive? Can I just go for the view without spending a lot?

A: Rooftop bars in Shanghai are generally expensive — cocktails typically cost ¥100–¥200 each, and many have a minimum spend or cover charge (¥100–¥300 per person). However, there are more affordable options: - Le Bar (Le Royal Meridien): More affordable than The Bund rooftop bars, with good city views - Hotel rooftop lounges: Many mid-range hotels have rooftop bars with lower prices - Happy hour: Some rooftop bars offer happy hour discounts (usually 5:00–7:00 PM) If you're on a budget, skip the rooftop bars and enjoy the free views from The Bund promenade and Lujiazui riverside instead. The views are just as spectacular, and you can spend your money on a nice dinner instead.

Q: Is it safe to walk around Shanghai at night?

A: Yes, Shanghai is generally a very safe city at night. The main tourist areas (The Bund, Nanjing Road, Lujiazui, Yu Garden) are well-lit, crowded, and patrolled by police. However, as with any big city, take standard precautions: keep an eye on your belongings in crowded areas, avoid dark and isolated alleys, and use licensed taxis or your private car for transportation after dark. Your private driver can also provide safety tips and ensure you get back to your hotel safely at the end of the evening.

Q: Can we do a night tour with kids?

A: Yes! A Shanghai night tour can be a magical experience for kids, especially the observation decks (they'll love the glass floors and high-speed elevators), the river cruise (boat rides are always fun for kids), and The Bund light show (synchronized music and lights are mesmerizing). However, keep in mind: - Most kids get tired by 9:00–10:00 PM — plan a shorter tour (3–4 hours) - Bring snacks and water for the kids - Make sure the car has child seats (request in advance) - Avoid very crowded areas on weekends if your kids are young - The observation decks can be cool at height — bring jackets A private car with child seats is the most comfortable and safe way to do a night tour with kids. Your driver can help with stroller storage and kid-friendly restaurant recommendations.
