Shanghai to Hangzhou Day Trip: Complete Guide 2026

September 7, 2026 16 min read By Shanghai Private Transfer

Just 180 km southwest of Shanghai, Hangzhou is one of China's most beloved tourist destinations — famous for the stunning West Lake, ancient Buddhist temples, lush tea plantations, and delicious local cuisine. A Hangzhou day trip from Shanghai is a perfect escape from the city, offering a blend of natural beauty, history, and culture.

In this complete guide, we cover everything you need to know for a Shanghai to Hangzhou day trip: transportation options, best attractions to visit, suggested itineraries, prices, local food, and insider tips.

Best Hangzhou Attractions for a Day Trip

1. West Lake (西湖)

  • The crown jewel of Hangzhou — a UNESCO World Heritage Site
  • Admission: Free (boat rides and some attractions charge separately)
  • Visit time: 2–4 hours
  • Highlights: Su Causeway, Bai Causeway, Broken Bridge, Leifeng Pagoda, Three Pools Mirroring the Moon, boat rides
  • Tip: Start early to avoid crowds; a boat ride (¥55–¥70 per person) is the best way to see the lake

2. Lingyin Temple (灵隐寺)

  • One of China's oldest and most important Buddhist temples — founded in 328 AD
  • Admission: ¥45 (Feilai Feng scenic area) + ¥30 (Lingyin Temple itself)
  • Visit time: 1.5–2 hours
  • Highlights: Feilai Feng (Flying Peak) rock carvings, Grand Hall of the Great Sage, ancient Buddhist statues, serene mountain setting
  • Tip: Visit in the morning for a peaceful atmosphere; dress modestly (shoulders and knees covered)

3. Longjing Tea Village (龙井村)

  • Home of China's most famous green tea — Longjing (Dragon Well) tea
  • Admission: Free (tea tasting and purchase optional)
  • Visit time: 1–1.5 hours
  • Highlights: Lush tea plantations, traditional tea houses, tea tasting, learning about tea processing, buying authentic Longjing tea
  • Tip: Best visited in spring (March–April) during tea picking season; try the tea at a local family-run tea house

4. Hefang Street (河坊街)

  • Historic commercial street with traditional architecture
  • Admission: Free
  • Visit time: 1–1.5 hours
  • Highlights: Traditional shops, local snacks, handicrafts, tea houses, street performers, traditional medicine shops
  • Tip: Best visited in the evening when lanterns are lit; try local snacks like West Lake lotus root starch, green tea cakes, and beggar's chicken

5. Leifeng Pagoda (雷峰塔)

  • Iconic pagoda on the south shore of West Lake
  • Admission: ¥40
  • Visit time: 45 min–1 hour
  • Highlights: Panoramic views of West Lake, ancient ruins of the original pagoda, elevator to the top, sunset views
  • Tip: Visit at sunset for stunning views of the lake; the pagoda is beautifully lit at night

6. China National Silk Museum (中国丝绸博物馆)

  • The largest silk museum in the world
  • Admission: Free (reservation recommended)
  • Visit time: 1–1.5 hours
  • Highlights: 5,000 years of silk history, ancient silk artifacts, live demonstrations of silk weaving, traditional costumes
  • Tip: Great for a rainy day or for history and culture enthusiasts; closed on Mondays

7. Xixi National Wetland Park (西溪国家湿地公园)

  • China's first national wetland park — a peaceful escape from the city
  • Admission: ¥80 (boat ride additional ¥60)
  • Visit time: 2–3 hours
  • Highlights: Scenic waterways, traditional villages, bird watching, boat rides, walking trails, seasonal flowers
  • Tip: Best visited in spring or autumn; take a boat ride to explore the wetland's hidden corners

Hangzhou Local Food to Try

  • Dongpo Pork (东坡肉) — Braised pork belly named after the poet Su Dongpo, tender and flavorful
  • West Lake Vinegar Fish (西湖醋鱼) — Freshwater fish in a sweet and sour vinegar sauce, a Hangzhou classic
  • Longjing Shrimp (龙井虾仁) — Fresh shrimp stir-fried with Longjing tea leaves, delicate and fragrant
  • Beggar's Chicken (叫花鸡) — Whole chicken wrapped in lotus leaves and clay, slow-roasted
  • West Lake Lotus Root Starch (西湖藕粉) — Sweet, translucent dessert made from lotus root starch
  • Green Tea Cakes (绿茶饼) — Crispy pastries filled with sweet green tea paste
  • Hangzhou Roast Duck (杭州烤鸭) — Similar to Peking duck but with local seasonings
  • Longjing Tea (龙井茶) — China's most famous green tea, best enjoyed fresh in a local tea house

Tips for a Great Hangzhou Day Trip

  1. Start early — West Lake and Lingyin Temple get crowded after 10 AM. Arrive early for the best experience and photos.
  2. Wear comfortable shoes — you'll do a lot of walking on causeways, temple steps, and historic streets.
  3. Bring cash — some smaller shops, tea houses, and snack stalls don't accept cards or mobile payment.
  4. Visit on a weekday if possible — weekends and holidays are very crowded, especially at West Lake.
  5. Try the local tea — Longjing tea is best tasted fresh at a village tea house. Avoid buying "antique" tea from tourist shops.
  6. Don't try to see everything — Hangzhou has enough attractions for 2–3 days. Pick 3–4 highlights for a day trip to avoid rushing.
  7. Bring an umbrella — Hangzhou is known for its rain, especially in spring and summer. A light rain can actually make West Lake more beautiful.
  8. Respect temple etiquette — at Lingyin Temple, dress modestly (shoulders and knees covered), keep voices low, and don't take photos inside prayer halls.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How far is Hangzhou from Shanghai?

A: Hangzhou is approximately 180 km southwest of Shanghai. By private car, the drive takes about 2–2.5 hours each way depending on traffic. By high-speed train, the journey is 45–60 minutes, but you need to add time for getting to/from the train stations.

Q: Can I do a Hangzhou day trip by high-speed train?

A: Yes, high-speed trains are fast and affordable. However, you'll need to arrange local transportation in Hangzhou (taxi, metro, or walking between attractions), and you'll have to carry your bags with you. For families and groups, a private car is more convenient and offers better value.

Q: How many attractions should I visit in one day?

A: We recommend visiting 3–4 main attractions in one day, such as Lingyin Temple, West Lake (with boat ride), Leifeng Pagoda, and Hefang Street. Trying to see more than 4 attractions can feel rushed and exhausting.

Q: Do I need to book attraction tickets in advance?

A: During peak season (April–May, October–November) and holidays, we recommend booking Lingyin Temple and Leifeng Pagoda tickets online in advance. West Lake is free and doesn't require tickets. Your driver or transfer company can help with bookings.

Q: Is Hangzhou wheelchair accessible?

A: Hangzhou's main streets and some areas of West Lake are wheelchair accessible, but the causeways, temple steps, and historic streets have many steps and uneven surfaces. Lingyin Temple in particular is not very wheelchair-friendly due to its mountain setting.

Q: Can I combine Hangzhou with Wuzhen or Xitang in one day?

A: We don't recommend trying to visit both Hangzhou and a water town in one day — it's too rushed. If you have 2 days, consider a multi-day private charter covering both. Alternatively, visit Hangzhou on one day and Wuzhen/Xitang on a separate day trip.

Q: What is the best time of year to visit Hangzhou?

A: The best times to visit Hangzhou are spring (March–May) when the tea plantations are green and flowers are blooming, and autumn (October–November) when the weather is cool and comfortable. Summer can be hot and humid, while winter is cold but less crowded.
