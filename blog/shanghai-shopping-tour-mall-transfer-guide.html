Shanghai Shopping Tour & Mall Transfer Guide 2026

September 7, 2026 12 min read By Shanghai Private Transfer

Shanghai is one of Asia's premier shopping destinations — a city where world-class luxury boutiques, sprawling modern malls, traditional markets, and bargain outlet centers coexist within a few kilometers. From the iconic neon-lit Nanjing Road to the tree-lined boulevards of the former French Concession, from the gleaming luxury malls of Lujiazui to the bargain-filled markets of Qipu Road, Shanghai offers an unforgettable shopping experience for every budget and style.

But Shanghai's best shopping destinations are spread across multiple districts — from Pudong to Puxi, from the city center to the suburbs. Navigating between them by taxi or metro can be time-consuming, especially when carrying multiple shopping bags. A private car with driver is the perfect solution for a Shanghai shopping tour.

In this complete guide, we cover the best Shanghai shopping districts and malls by category, suggested shopping tour itineraries, and why a private car is the ideal way to shop in Shanghai.

Shanghai Shopping Districts Guide

1. Nanjing Road (南京东路 & 南京西路)

  • Vibe: Iconic, bustling, the world's longest shopping street
  • Best for: Mid-range brands, department stores, souvenir shopping, people-watching
  • Nanjing East Road (南京东路): The famous pedestrian shopping street stretching from The Bund to People's Square. Home to flagship stores, traditional Chinese brands, souvenir shops, and massive department stores (No.1 Department Store, Shanghai Fashion Store, Yong'an Department Store). Very touristy but a must-visit for first-time shoppers.
  • Nanjing West Road (南京西路): The more upscale section, from People's Square to Jing'an Temple. Home to luxury malls (Plaza 66, Citic Square, Kerry Centre), high-end boutiques, and international brands. Less touristy than Nanjing East Road.
  • Notable malls: Plaza 66 (luxury), Kerry Centre (upscale), Citic Square (mid-to-upscale), HKRI Taikoo Hui (lifestyle & luxury), No.1 Department Store (traditional)
  • Tip: Start at The Bund end of Nanjing East Road and walk west. The street is pedestrianized, so wear comfortable shoes. For luxury shopping, head to Nanjing West Road near Jing'an Temple.

2. Huaihai Road (淮海路) & Former French Concession

  • Vibe: Tree-lined, elegant, trendy, European atmosphere
  • Best for: Mid-to-upscale brands, trendy boutiques, lifestyle shopping, cafes
  • Huaihai Road: Shanghai's answer to the Champs-Élysées — a tree-lined boulevard stretching from Xintiandi to Xujiahui. Home to international brand flagships, upscale malls, and trendy boutiques. Less crowded than Nanjing Road and more sophisticated.
  • Former French Concession side streets: Wander along Anfu Road, Wukang Road, and Yongkang Road for independent boutiques, vintage shops, art galleries, and concept stores.
  • Notable malls: IAPM (upscale, open late), Huaihai 755 (lifestyle), K11 (art & shopping), Shanghai Times Square (mid-range)
  • Tip: IAPM Mall on Huaihai Road is open until 10 PM and has a great mix of international brands, restaurants, and a cinema. Perfect for evening shopping.

3. Lujiazui (陆家嘴) — Pudong

  • Vibe: Modern, gleaming, skyscraper-filled, luxury-focused
  • Best for: Luxury brands, high-end malls, skyline views, upscale dining
  • Lujiazui: Shanghai's financial district, home to the city's most iconic skyscrapers (Shanghai Tower, Jin Mao Tower, SWFC) and some of its most luxurious malls. Perfect for high-end shoppers who want to combine luxury shopping with skyline views.
  • Notable malls: IFC Mall (Shanghai's most luxurious mall, all major luxury brands), Super Brand Mall (large, mid-to-upscale, family-friendly), L+Mall (newer, upscale), Shanghai Tower Mall (luxury & lifestyle)
  • Tip: IFC Mall in Lujiazui is Shanghai's premier luxury shopping destination — home to Apple, Hermès, Louis Vuitton, Chanel, Gucci, and every major luxury brand. Combine a shopping trip with a visit to the Shanghai Tower observation deck or a riverside walk along the Bund promenade across the river.

4. Xintiandi (新天地) & Xuhui

  • Vibe: Restored shikumen (stone-gate) houses, upscale, trendy, lifestyle-focused
  • Best for: Boutique shopping, lifestyle brands, art galleries, upscale dining
  • Xintiandi: A beautifully restored area of traditional shikumen houses converted into upscale boutiques, restaurants, bars, and galleries. More about lifestyle and experience than mass-market shopping. Perfect for a leisurely afternoon of browsing, dining, and people-watching.
  • Tianzifang (田子坊): A maze of narrow alleyways in the former French Concession, filled with independent boutiques, craft shops, art studios, and cafes. Great for unique souvenirs and handmade goods.
  • Notable shopping: Xintiandi Style (lifestyle mall), Xintiandi North Block (boutiques), Tianzifang (independent shops), Sinan Mansions (luxury & lifestyle)
  • Tip: Tianzifang is best visited on a weekday — weekends can be extremely crowded. Bargain politely at smaller shops, as prices are often negotiable.

5. Qipu Road (七浦路) & Bargain Markets

  • Vibe: Bustling, chaotic, bargain-filled, wholesale
  • Best for: Cheap clothing, accessories, knockoffs, wholesale shopping, bargaining
  • Qipu Road Clothing Market: Shanghai's largest wholesale clothing market — multiple massive buildings filled with thousands of stalls selling cheap clothing, accessories, shoes, and knockoff brands. Prices are very low and bargaining is expected. Popular with budget shoppers and wholesale buyers.
  • Other bargain markets: Xiangyang Market (electronics & gadgets), Yuyuan Bazaar (traditional Chinese goods & souvenirs), Dongtai Road Antique Market (antiques & collectibles)
  • Tip: Qipu Road is best visited in the morning (before 11 AM) when it's less crowded and vendors are more open to bargaining. Start at 50–60% of the asking price and work up. Wear comfortable shoes and keep your valuables secure — the market can be crowded and chaotic.

6. Outlet Malls (郊区奥特莱斯)

  • Vibe: Suburban, spacious, discount-focused, brand-name outlets
  • Best for: Discounted brand-name clothing, accessories, sporting goods, family shopping
  • Shanghai has several large outlet malls in the suburbs, offering discounts of 30–70% on brand-name goods:
  • Qingpu Outlets (百联奥特莱斯): Shanghai's largest and most popular outlet mall, located in Qingpu District near Dianshan Lake. Over 200 brand stores including Nike, Adidas, Coach, Michael Kors, Armani, and more. About 45 minutes from the city center by car.
  • Florentia Village (佛罗伦萨小镇): An Italian-themed outlet mall in Pudong, near Pudong Airport. Over 200 luxury and premium brands including Prada, Gucci, Burberry, and more. Beautiful architecture and good dining options. About 30 minutes from Lujiazui by car.
  • Fox Town Outlets (狐狸城): A smaller outlet mall in Songjiang District, popular for sporting goods and casual brands.
  • Tip: Outlet malls are best visited on weekdays — weekends can be very crowded, especially during sales holidays. A private car is essential for outlet shopping, as they're located in the suburbs and not easily accessible by public transit. Your driver can wait while you shop and store all your purchases in the car.

7. Xujiahui (徐家汇)

  • Vibe: Busy, commercial, electronics-focused, university area
  • Best for: Electronics, computers, gadgets, mid-range malls, books
  • Xujiahui: A major commercial and transportation hub in southwestern Shanghai, home to several large malls and the famous Xujiahui Cathedral. Best known for electronics shopping — multiple multi-story electronics malls selling computers, phones, cameras, and gadgets at competitive prices.
  • Notable malls: Grand Gateway (large, mid-to-upscale), Pacific Department Store, Metro City (electronics & entertainment), Bailian Xujiahui Shopping Center
  • Tip: For electronics shopping, head to Metro City or the various electronics markets around Xujiahui. Bargain politely and compare prices across multiple stalls. Make sure to check warranty and return policies before purchasing.

Tips for a Great Shanghai Shopping Tour

  1. Book a full-day private car — Shanghai's best shopping is spread across multiple districts. A full-day car (8–10 hours) lets you shop from mall to mall without worrying about transportation or carrying bags.
  2. Leave shopping bags in the car — The biggest advantage of a private car for shopping is bag storage. Leave your purchases in the vehicle as you go from mall to mall, and pick them up at the end of the day. No carrying heavy bags around!
  3. Plan your itinerary by district — Group malls by area to minimize travel time: Lujiazui in the morning, Nanjing West Road in the afternoon, Xintiandi for evening. For outlet shopping, dedicate a half or full day as they're in the suburbs.
  4. Ask your driver for recommendations — Local drivers know the best malls, the current sales, and can help avoid tourist traps. They can also help with store locations and restaurant recommendations within malls.
  5. Wear comfortable shoes — Shanghai malls are massive (some have 8+ floors) and you'll do a lot of walking. Wear comfortable shoes and take breaks between malls in the car.
  6. Bring your passport for tax refunds — Foreign tourists can get a VAT refund (up to 11%) on purchases at participating stores. Look for the "Tax Free" sign, spend over ¥500 in a single store in one day, and keep your receipts. Your driver can help direct you to tax refund counters at the airport.
  7. Bargain at markets, not at malls — Bargaining is expected at Qipu Road, Tianzifang, and other markets (start at 50–60% of asking price). At正规 malls and department stores, prices are fixed and non-negotiable.
  8. Check store return policies — Before making large purchases, check the return and exchange policy. Most malls offer 7-day returns for unworn items with receipts, but policies vary by store.
  9. Avoid peak shopping times — Weekends and Chinese holidays (Golden Week, Spring Festival) are extremely crowded at all major malls. If possible, shop on weekdays for a more relaxed experience.
  10. Stay hydrated and take breaks — Shopping in Shanghai can be exhausting, especially in summer heat. Take breaks in the car between malls, drink plenty of water, and enjoy a leisurely lunch to recharge.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the best time of year for shopping in Shanghai?

A: Shanghai is a great shopping destination year-round, but the best sales are during: - Chinese New Year (January–February): Pre-holiday sales and Spring Festival promotions - Golden Week (October 1–7): National Day sales at many malls - Double 11 (November 11): China's biggest online shopping festival, with many malls also offering in-store promotions - End of season (January & July): Winter and summer clearance sales Weekdays are less crowded than weekends for a more relaxed shopping experience.

Q: Can I get a tax refund on my shopping in Shanghai?

A: Yes! Foreign tourists can get a VAT refund (up to 11%) on purchases at participating stores in Shanghai. To qualify: - Spend over ¥500 in a single store in one day - Look for the "Tax Free" sign in the store - Keep your receipts and get a tax refund form from the store - Claim your refund at the airport before departure (PVG or SHA) Your driver can help direct you to participating stores and explain the process. Allow extra time at the airport for tax refund processing.

Q: Are outlet malls worth the trip from the city center?

A: Yes, especially if you're looking for discounted brand-name goods. Qingpu Outlets and Florentia Village offer discounts of 30–70% on brands like Nike, Adidas, Coach, Michael Kors, Armani, and more. They're located 30–45 minutes from the city center, so a half-day or full-day trip is recommended. A private car is the most convenient way to get there, as public transit options are limited and you'll have shopping bags to carry.

Q: Is Qipu Road Market safe for tourists?

A: Qipu Road is generally safe for tourists, but it's a crowded, chaotic wholesale market — take standard precautions: keep your wallet and phone secure, don't carry large amounts of cash, and be aware of your surroundings. Bargain politely but firmly, and inspect merchandise carefully before purchasing. The market is best visited in the morning when it's less crowded. Your driver can wait nearby and help with any language issues.

Q: Can I use my credit card for shopping in Shanghai?

A: International credit cards (Visa, Mastercard, American Express) are accepted at most large malls, department stores, and luxury boutiques. However, smaller shops, market stalls, and some restaurants may only accept Chinese mobile payment (Alipay or WeChat Pay) or cash. We recommend setting up Alipay or WeChat Pay on your phone before your trip, and carrying some Chinese Yuan (¥) cash for smaller purchases. Your driver can help with mobile payment setup if needed.

Q: How much should I budget for a shopping day in Shanghai?

A: Shopping budgets vary widely depending on your style: - Bargain/market shopping: ¥200–¥1,000 per day - Mid-range mall shopping: ¥1,000–¥5,000 per day - Luxury/high-end shopping: ¥5,000–¥50,000+ per day - Outlet mall shopping: ¥1,000–¥5,000 per day Plus the cost of your private car transfer (¥800–¥1,100 for a full day) and meals. Set a budget before you go and prioritize the items you most want to buy.

Q: Can my driver help me with shopping or carrying bags?

A: Your driver is there to provide transportation and local recommendations, but they're not personal shopping assistants. They can wait in the car while you shop, store your bags in the vehicle, and help with directions and restaurant recommendations. For heavy or bulky purchases, your driver can help load them into the car. If you need a dedicated shopping assistant or interpreter, we can arrange one for an additional fee (typically ¥500–¥800/day).

Q: What should I do if I buy too much to carry on the plane?

A: If you exceed your airline's baggage allowance, you have several options: - Pay for extra baggage at the airport check-in counter - Ship items home — Many malls and stores offer international shipping services (DHL, FedEx, EMS). Your driver can help direct you to shipping services. - Buy an extra suitcase — Affordable suitcases are available at Qipu Road and many malls. - Use a luggage delivery service — Some services will pick up your luggage from your hotel and deliver it to the airport. Plan ahead and check your airline's baggage policy before your shopping trip to avoid surprises at the airport.
